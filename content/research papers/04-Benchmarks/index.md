# 04 — Benchmarks

Papers that define how we measure LLM capability — from broad task suites and real-world coding challenges to live human preference ratings.

> New to this material? Start with the beginner-friendly walkthrough in [explanations/](./explanations/00-start-here.md), which explains every paper here in plain language with diagrams, examples, and a linked [glossary](./explanations/glossary.md).

---

## Papers

| # | Title | Year | Link | Notes |
|---|-------|------|------|-------|
| 1 | **Beyond the Imitation Game: Quantifying and Extrapolating the Capabilities of Language Models** (BIG-Bench) | 2022 | [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.04615) | First large-scale collaborative open benchmark across 200+ diverse tasks |
| 2 | **SWE-bench: Can Language Models Resolve Real-World GitHub Issues?** | 2023 | [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.06770) | Measures real software engineering ability on actual GitHub issues |
| 3 | **Chatbot Arena: An Open Platform for Evaluating LLMs by Human Preference** | 2024 | [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.04132) | Live Elo-style leaderboard based on blind head-to-head human votes |

---

*These three benchmarks together cover breadth (BIG-Bench), real-world utility (SWE-bench), and human preference (Chatbot Arena).*
